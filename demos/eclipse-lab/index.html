Make the Sun disappear.

One Moon. One Sun. Can you line them up to make a total solar eclipse?

A quick classroom investigation

Learning goal: explain that a solar eclipse happens when the Moon moves between Earth and the Sun, blocking our view of the Sun.

Before: ask students to predict what will cover the Sun. During: have a student move the slider on your whiteboard and collect two observations. After: ask pairs to explain why the Sun still exists when it disappears from view.

This original portfolio sample is aimed at ages 9–12. It is not a full lesson or the client’s approved safety content. The view models apparent discs from one observer on Earth; sizes, movement and timing are simplified. The sky illustration is expressive, not a brightness measurement.
